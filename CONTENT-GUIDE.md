# Content guide

Fill this out in plain language for each project before touching the HTML. Then it's just copy-paste into `projects/case-study-template.html`.

## Picking your projects
Choose 3–6. For each, you should be able to answer: what was the problem, what did I actually do (not the team — you), and what changed as a result. If you can't answer the third one, pick a different project or focus on process/learning instead of outcome.

## Per-project worksheet

**Project name:**
**Company / context:**
**Your role (be specific — "designed the onboarding flow," not "UX designer"):**
**Timeline:**
**Team (who else was involved, what did they own):**

1. **Problem** — What was broken, confusing, or missing? Who felt it? What evidence do you have (data, complaints, a stakeholder ask)?

2. **Research & constraints** — What did you learn before designing? Interviews, analytics, competitive review? What limited your options (tech debt, timeline, business rules, accessibility requirements)?

3. **Process** — This is the section people skip writing and shouldn't. Pick 2-3 key decisions and show the before/after thinking: what did you try first, what was wrong with it, what changed your mind. Screenshots of rough sketches or early Figma frames are more convincing here than polish.

4. **Solution** — Walk through the final flow. For each key screen, one sentence on the decision behind it, not just a description of what's visible.

5. **Outcome** — What happened after it shipped? Numbers if you have them. If not, qualitative signal (user feedback, stakeholder reaction) is fine — just be honest about what you don't know.

6. **Reflection** — One honest thing you'd do differently. This signals maturity more than any amount of polish.

## Tone
Write like you're explaining it to a smart colleague, not pitching. Avoid "leveraged," "synergy," "revolutionized." Specific beats impressive.
